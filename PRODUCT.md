# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Life OS is a private, single-user system for Mehdi. He uses it from a Windows PC and an Android phone while rebuilding structure around deen, employment, driving-license training, health, and creative work. The primary job is to know what matters now, record what actually happened, and recover quickly when a plan breaks.

## Product Purpose

Life OS turns important commitments into a flexible daily operating system. It anchors the day around salah, protects Quran recitation, makes driving practice and job-search actions visible, supports consistent training, and gives YouTube and experiments bounded space. Success means Mehdi can open the app in seconds, see the next meaningful action, log it with minimal friction, and understand whether a week produced consistency and real-world outcomes.

## Brand Personality

Alive, encouraging, and grounded. The interface should feel clean and capable like a well-made modern tool, but never sterile. Expressive color, friendly emoji, responsive motion, streaks, and a soft 3D companion should make the system feel personal and full of life.

## Anti-references

- Sterile black-and-white dashboards that imitate developer tools without warmth or personality.
- Corporate productivity software filled with tables, configuration, and management language.
- Childish gamification that awards meaningless points for tapping buttons.
- Shame-based failure screens, aggressive red warnings, or language that treats a missed day as personal failure.
- Dense dashboards that require analysis before the user knows what to do next.
- Decorative glass panels, endless identical cards, and animation that delays routine actions.

## Design Principles

1. Show the next meaningful action before showing analytics.
2. Be flexible about timing but firm about explicit commitments.
3. Record truth, including skipped and missed work, without turning honesty into punishment.
4. Reward recovery and return, not only perfect streaks.
5. Measure outcomes where outcomes matter, especially applications, replies, interviews, publications, and income.
6. Treat deen respectfully as the foundation of the day, not a source of points or decorative gamification.
7. Keep daily logging fast enough to use from a phone between real activities.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Text must remain readable at small mobile sizes, color must never be the only status signal, all core actions must work with keyboard and touch, and tap targets must be comfortably sized. The normal experience may use expressive motion, but reduced-motion mode must preserve meaning without movement. The interface should support English first while keeping future Arabic and right-to-left support structurally possible.
